# Docking you can see: clamps, signal lights and floodlights at every berth

- Each berth's docking collar now has four clamps on hinges round its rim. They stand open like petals while a ship comes in, then after contact swing shut one after another until their jaws meet the ship's nose, each landing on the clunk you hear. A slim ship's nose is gripped further in than a broad one's. Before a ship leaves, they open again.
- A ring of lit signal panels round each collar's face: amber running round the ring while a ship comes in or goes out, steady amber at contact, and green once every clamp is home and the seal holds.
- Floodlights on the clamps' housings shine out along the arm at the approach while a berth has a ship coming, berthed or leaving.
- The collar has a mating plate with its hatch across the ring, where a ship's nose meets it.
- The berth shot of the arrival runs three seconds longer, so you see the clamps close and the ring turn green before you go through the lock.
