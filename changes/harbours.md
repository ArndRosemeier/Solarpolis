# Harbours on every water

- The boats now lie in harbours round the ring lake, the great lake and the smaller lakes, where the paths come down to the shore: a stone landing, a timber pier on slender bronze piles, a floating pontoon with fingers between the berths, tall guide piles, cleats, lamp bollards and a swimming ladder.
- Each boat lies stern to the pontoon, bow out. Going sailing, it motors quietly straight out to open water and makes sail; coming back, it drops its sail, turns on the spot and backs into its berth.
- You can walk out along the pier and onto the pontoon; no reeds grow where the boats lie.
- The ring lake's waterline is where the water really is, a few metres in from its bank, so berths are always deep enough.
