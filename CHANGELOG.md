# Changelog

Every merge to main is the next version, v0.001 and up by 0.001. The notes come from `changes/` (see changes/README.md); never edit this file by hand.

## v0.156 — Transport capsules

- Open your link (Tab) and call a capsule: a small glass pod flies in, sets down on a stop near you and opens its door. Click it to get in.
- You walk round to the door, step up, sit down, and choose where to go from "Ride to…": every court and landmark has a stop kept clear for capsules.
- The ride climbs straight up, crosses high over the land and comes straight down, never faster than is comfortable; the pod banks into the gravity you feel, and the gravity meter says you are in a capsule.
- Hold the right button to look round while you ride; at the stop you stand up and step out, and the pod waits, or flies off when you send it away.

## v0.155 — Residents dress in the Commonwear of 3026

- Screen fabrics glow in each wearer's own colour and their aurora slowly moves; people farther off keep the same colours.

## v0.152 — The boats have crews

- Every boat is sailed by its own residents, the same people every time, who live under the court nearest its harbour.
- On a day their boat goes out they come up out of their court's lift together, walk to the harbour, out along the pier and the pontoon, and step aboard over the stern before it casts off.
- Aboard, each has their place: in the cockpit or on the trampolines when the boat motors or goes about, hiked out on the windward side under sail, crossing over at every tack. A foiler lifts them with it.
- Back in the berth they step off and walk home. On days their boat stays in, they stay below.
- Go → "Fly: beside the nearest crew on their way to their boat".

## v0.146 — Swallows, swifts and starling murmurations

- Swallows nest under the bridges' decks and hawk low over the rivers and meadows round them all day, twittering, skimming the water and lifting over the trees; now and then one flies home to its nest, and at night they sleep there.
- Swifts live at the Lift Stair. By day they feed high over the floor; in the sunset's light they race screaming round the foot of the Stair in small parties, then climb toward the axis, where they spend the night circling slowly in the thin air and weak gravity, and come down with the light.
- Starlings roost in their thousands in a wood by every lake. They leave in parties at first light to feed on the grassland round about, gather toward dusk in a murmuration over the water that ripples and folds as it wheels, drift over the roost and pour down into the trees as the light goes. The rush of its wings and the roost's chatter can be heard from far off, and a murmuration can be seen as a dark smudge on the sky kilometres away.
- Blackbirds, swallows and starlings have a sheen up close: the blackbird's black is no longer flat, and the starling's plumage glints purple and green and is spangled with pale spots.
- Debug menu: fly beside the nearest swallow, swift or starling (the eye rides along with it for a few seconds), before or into the nearest murmuration; jump to the starlings' dusk or the swifts' race.
- The lunar passenger tour's test moved to the slow suite (planning a tour takes over a second), so the fast checks stay under their 30 s.

## v0.144 — Wind over the floor, and boats that sail

- The habitat now has a wind near the floor: its own steady breeze, which swings and veers a little over a few days, plus gusts that sweep across the water. By day the land under the sunline's bright stretch draws the air along the axis toward it, so the breeze shifts as the light travels from the wild end to the port end.
- About ninety sail boats belong to the reference habitat's water: two-person skiffs with wing sails, foiling trimarans that lift out of the water once they are fast enough, and glass-roofed catamarans. They sail on the ring lake (round the cylinder from one side of the pavilion to the other) and on the lakes on the floor.
- Each boat goes out on some days, in daylight, more often in a good breeze. It races round marks, cruises, or makes a long passage round the ring lake. Upwind it tacks and downwind it gybes, as a sailor would, and it lies head to wind at its mooring.
- The spin is felt: a boat sailing with the spin is heavier and one sailing against it is lighter, so a foiler lifts off sooner sailing against the spin. On a small habitat the difference is plain.
- The boats are not drawn yet; that comes next. Debug menu: a line shows the wind where you are and how many boats are under sail, and Go → "Fly: beside the nearest boat under sail" takes you to one.

## v0.143 — Birds you can see and hear

- The woods have birds: blackbirds, robins, wrens, song thrushes, woodpeckers and wood pigeons, each living round its own tree and singing from the tree tops near it. Every bird call you hear now comes from a bird you can find.
- At dawn the chorus rolls along the cylinder with the light; by day the birds sit, look about, hop and peck on the ground and fly between their trees; at night they roost in the crowns.
- Skylarks rise singing from the meadows in slow spirals, hang high up for minutes and drop the last metres with wings closed. Tawny owls hoot and fly between their perches at night.
- Up close a bird has single flight feathers that beat (the hand lagging the arm), fold as it lands, a tail that fans and a beak that opens as it sings, in its species' plumage; far off it becomes a simple shape and then a speck, so it never pops.
- Flight follows the spin: a bird is heavier flying spinward and lighter against the spin, is pushed sideways climbing or diving, and beats slower in the weaker gravity toward the axis.
- Debug menu: fly beside the nearest bird, lark or owl, and jump to the dawn chorus or nightfall.

## v0.117 — The air drags, and the feet feel your weight

- The air turns with the habitat, so anything moving through it is slowed: a ball thrown hard curves down sooner, a long fall tops out at a skydiver's speed, and the air is thinner (less drag) toward the axis.
- On foot the legs now push through their grip, which is friction times the weight you feel. Running with the spin you grip harder; against the spin, or high up toward the axis, you skid more and need longer to speed up or stop. A fast run feels the headwind. On an endcap slope the Coriolis force pushes you sideways and your legs hold the line.
- Walking and running take a moment to get up to speed and to stop (the 40 m/s run about 4 s). The debug menu's Travel 10× faster also makes the legs grip harder, so boosted runs still start and stop quickly.

## v0.102 — Running fast no longer throws you into the air

- Running fast over the floor no longer launches you into long, floaty jumps. Seams between the ground's triangles, kerbs and river banks could pop the body up a few centimetres, and that pop became a launch of 10 m/s or more; now the ground can stop you but never throw you.
- Running into a river at speed stops you in the water within a few metres, as water does, instead of skimming across and ramping off the far bank.
- Running downhill keeps you on the slope instead of sailing out over it.
- What stays is real: in a spinning habitat, running against the spin makes you lighter and running with it heavier. At a full run (40 m/s) in the reference cylinder a jump against the spin hangs about twice as long as one with it.
