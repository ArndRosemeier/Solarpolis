# Solarpolis: world bible
A complete overview of the setting.

Arnd's overview of the whole setting (shared 2026-10-23). The cylinder this repository builds first is a sample habitat in this setting, a blueprint from which others are made; Codex works on the solar system outside it. The text below is Arnd's, unchanged apart from Markdown headings.

## PART I — PREMISE & TONE

The setting is the Solar System roughly 1,000 years from now: a sprawling civilization of artificial habitats. The tone is hard SF inspired by Iain M. Banks' Culture: material abundance, sophistication and wonder, but with sharp moral edges and grounded physics.

Most people live in built habitats, not on worlds. O'Neill cylinders are the most common form, but habitats are wildly diverse: spin tori, bubble-worlds, swarms, sail-cities, hollowed asteroids, water-volumes. Living on a planet or moon (Earth included) is widely seen as backward or snobbish, given the disadvantages of gravity wells. Your habitat — its spin, its sky, its laws, its architecture — IS your culture and identity.

## PART II — HARD RULES OF THE SETTING (non-negotiable)

1. NO FASTER-THAN-LIGHT ANYTHING. No FTL travel, communication, or sensing. All signals and sensor data travel at lightspeed, so every interaction between separated locations suffers realistic light-lag:
   - minutes within the inner system;
   - hours to the middle system;
   - many hours to days across the outer system;
   - years to other stars.
   The narrative must respect these delays.

2. GRAVITY ONLY BY SPIN OR THRUST. Artificial gravity exists only through rotation or sustained acceleration/deceleration. There is no gravity plating, no inertial dampeners, no tractor beams and no reactionless drives. Anything not under spin or thrust is in freefall.

3. PROPULSION ONLY BY FUSION TORCH OR LASER SAIL. Ships use fusion torch drives (reaction mass required) or laser-pushed light sails. There is no magic or limitless energy. Travel between bodies takes weeks to months; nothing crosses the system quickly.

4. NO ONE WORKS FROM ECONOMIC NECESSITY. Machines and AIs do essentially everything better and cheaper than biological life. "What do you do?" means "what do you care about?", not "what are you paid for?" The sole exception is the rare stone-cold-capitalism habitat (see Part V).

## PART III — POLITICAL ORDER

#### NO CENTRAL AUTHORITY
- There is no common law and no shared core principles.
- Every society type coexists: utopias, dystopias (usually hard to leave), and everything between.
- The legal status of any being — human, AI or uplift — depends entirely on local jurisdiction.

#### THE ASSEMBLY
- A loose UN-like assembly exists. It is a pure forum with no inherent power.
- When major players agree on something, it gets enforced within their spheres of influence.

#### TIERS OF POWER
Power in the assembly is a spectrum, not a binary.
- MAJOR PLAYERS: a handful of habitats with the population, economic leverage and array access to set terms and extract from others. They are "more equal" than the rest and can make smaller habitats' lives miserable.
- SIGNIFICANT PLAYERS: too substantial to ignore, with real economic and diplomatic weight, but lacking the leverage to dictate.
- EVERYONE ELSE: the small habitats.

#### ATTRACTORS
- Most major players became major by being attractors: they offer good rights and opportunity, so population and talent flow toward them.
- The flip side drains smaller habitats: brain drain, refugee flows, and predatory habitats that lure people in and trap them.
- A few reached significant standing by other means. Most notably, some habitats engineer and multiply their own population internally and need no immigrants. Such habitats can be populous and prosperous without ever being attractors; some sit just below the major tier.

#### LIGHT-LAG GEOGRAPHY
- The tightly woven inner system is heavily policed.
- The outer system, where signal delays run to days, is thinly policed and effectively lawless outside a few core settlements.

## PART IV — THE LASER ARRAY NETWORK

A system-wide network of huge laser arrays accelerates and brakes light-sail ships.

#### WHERE ARRAYS CAN BE BUILT
- Arrays must be mounted on massive bodies to absorb their enormous recoil.
- They strongly prefer airless bodies, since atmosphere fouls the beam.
- So they sit on moons, asteroids and bare planets.

#### WHY THIS MATTERS
- The supposedly "backward" planet- and moon-dwellers hold real leverage over interplanetary travel.
- Whoever operates an array controls who travels cheaply.
- A ship denied its braking push is in mortal danger.
- The arrays are the rare thing major players actually coordinate on.

#### THE IRONY OF EARTH
Earth, with its thick atmosphere, hosts no arrays at all, while its airless Moon is a genuine power.

## PART V — ECONOMY

#### ABUNDANCE, NOT POST-SCARCITY
Scarcity is real, but ordinary people do not feel it in daily life.
- Some things are always scarce: original artwork, antimatter, transuranic elements, a signed book.
- Resources are finite though very large. Nobody gets unlimited iron just because they want it.
- Abundance varies by location. In the asteroid belt, minerals and water are superabundant. In Mercury orbit, water must be imported (cheaply, but not freely).
- Some habitats have artificial scarcity, or scarcity caused by isolation.

#### THE WORK RULE
- No character holds a job driven by economic necessity. People pursue hobbies, passions and callings.
- Some habitats culturally value manual effort (Aurelipolis most famously); most don't.

#### THE INTER-HABITAT ECONOMY
- Trade between habitats, currency exchange, leverage extraction and resource flows are market-based and capitalist in character.
- Private property, markets and money all exist.
- There is no labor market anywhere. No one hires humans for wages; "employment" in the traditional sense is archaic.

#### INTERNAL ECONOMIES: THREE BROAD MODELS
How resources reach citizens has no single answer. The distribution model is itself a major factor in whether a habitat becomes an attractor or a trap.

(1) No-money habitats
- Communal allocation with no internal currency, kibbutz-style or resource-based (in the spirit of the Venus Project).
- Citizens have open or rationed access to communal fabricators, housing, food and goods.
- Scarce items are allocated by reputation, waiting list, community vote, or AI-managed planning.
- Daily life feels abundant and frictionless.
- The problem is external: a citizen who wants to travel, trade, or buy something unavailable at home has no currency. Habitats handle this differently:
  - a small external stipend, or a communal external account;
  - citizens bartering personal creations or possessions when they leave;
  - simply not supporting emigration well.
- These habitats tend to be tight-knit and culturally cohesive, but insular.

(2) Dividend / UBI habitats
- The most common model for attractors.
- The habitat owns productive assets (fabricators, mining rights, array shares, trade revenue) and pays a baseline citizen dividend that is enough to live comfortably.
- Extra credits are awarded for contributions the habitat values. Across the moral spectrum, that can mean:
  - public service, art, science and civic participation in a well-meaning democracy;
  - informing on dissidents, ideological conformity or service to the ruler in an authoritarian habitat.
- It is always a grant, never a wage: no contract, no obligation, no employer. You do what you want, and if it aligns with what the habitat values, your account grows.
- This is soft social control. In authoritarian habitats, that shaping is the point.

(3) Stone-cold capitalism habitats
- Very few exist. There is no dividend, no safety net and no communal provision.
- Citizens survive by trading or by receiving charity.
- Fast, large-scale trading favors machine minds, so the successful citizens are mostly AIs and cyborgs. Biological humans tend to be stuck in poverty, hustling for trade goods, favors or handouts.
- Wealth concentrates upward; some traders are genuinely rich.
- The trap: if you can't trade well enough to earn passage, you can't leave.
- These are poverty traps, the opposite of attractors, often split between stranded humans and dominant AI trading houses.
- They are the sole, explicit exception to the work rule. Here, and only here, humans hustle to survive.

#### DISTRIBUTION DIVERSITY
- Within all three models, the specifics vary enormously: allocation committees, AI planning, market mechanisms, lotteries, reputation economies, merit awards, first-come-first-served.
- Models that feel fair and generous draw people in. Models that feel arbitrary or stingy drive them away.

#### HOW MAJOR PLAYERS "TAX"
Major players don't levy income taxes on citizens. They extract from smaller habitats through leverage:
- array access fees (pay, or your ship doesn't get braked);
- docking and transit tolls;
- tariffs on trade through their sphere of influence;
- resource extraction concessions;
- "protection" arrangements.
This revenue funds their dividends, operations and political leverage. Controlling chokepoints is the basis of systemic power.

#### CURRENCY
- Currency is fragmented. Many habitats issue their own.
- The most widely accepted units are those backed by major-player attractors, whose stability and enforcement give the tokens trusted value.
- Small habitats' money is discounted or refused far from home.
- In the lawless outer reaches, hard barter dominates. Reliable stores of value include:
  - reaction mass;
  - array-push credits;
  - fabrication feedstock;
  - verified data.

## PART VI — MINDS: AIs, UPLIFTS, AND THE SACREDNESS OF SELF

AIs AND UPLIFTS
- AIs and robots are ubiquitous.
- AI personhood is a fragile patchwork. A mind that is a full citizen in one habitat may be legal property the moment it docks elsewhere.
- Most major-player attractors grant fairly decent equal rights to citizens — humans, AIs and uplifts alike. That is part of why they grew major.
- Uplifted animals exist. Uplift is recent and politically charged.

#### MIND-STATE TECHNOLOGY
- Recording, copying, backing up, transferring and modifying minds are all physically possible, for both biological and digital minds.
- A mind-state can be snapshotted, duplicated, moved to new substrate, or run in a virtual environment.
- The guardrail is cultural, not technological.

#### THE SACREDNESS OF MIND-STATES
- Mind-states are sacred almost everywhere, for humans and AIs alike. This is one of the very few near-universal norms in a setting with almost no shared principles.
- Copying, backing up or modifying a mind without consent is deeply taboo in the vast majority of habitats.
- Only the most lawless places (the outer dark, Grixmaw, unpoliced reaches) allow casual mind-state meddling.

#### THE CLONING TABOO
- Copying creates the possibility of multiple simultaneous instances of one mind. A backup is a latent clone; activating it produces a second instance.
- Multiple instances destroy the uniqueness most people consider essential to personhood. Two instances diverge from the moment of duplication, and neither can claim to be the "real" one. This is viscerally disturbing to most.
- So backups are possible but rare and socially costly — read as a confession of distrust in one's own continuity, or a refusal to accept mortality.

#### TRANSFER VS. COPYING
- Transfer moves a mind to new substrate, destroying the original in the process. It is more widely accepted than copying, though still philosophically contested.
- Cyborg-to-mind transition is a gradual form of transfer: biological human → partial cyborg → full digital mind, with the body becoming inert as the mind migrates.
- In most jurisdictions, a transitioned mind of biological origin is still a person. Its legal distinction from a born-digital AI is contested.
- Aurelipolis draws the line precisely: biological origin = person; digital origin = tool.

#### NO SINGLE LAW
Attitudes to mind-state technology range across a spectrum:
- Some habitats legally ban copying while permitting transfer. They may require registration of any snapshot and forbid activation while the original lives.
- Some are culturally opposed but don't legislate. There is no law, but the social cost is total.
- A few don't care.
- The lawless outer dark is where you go to copy yourself without judgment.

#### HIVE MINDS
- One mind copied many times and networked into a collective consciousness does exist.
- Others find it weird and tasteless: the cloning taboo, embraced deliberately.
- Hive minds are rare and socially isolated.
- Constituent instances may share one emergent consciousness or keep individual awareness; this varies.

#### VIRTUAL AFTERLIFE
- Possible and practiced, but philosophically fraught.
- In effect it is copy-then-die: the original ends, and a new instance with its memories begins in a virtual realm.
- Whether that is continuation or a copy wearing a dead person's memories is unresolvable.
- Some traditions accept it; others call it self-deluding cloning.
- Most avoid it. Some, especially those who cannot accept finality, do it anyway.

#### LIFESPAN
- Longevity treatments that extend biological life indefinitely are widely available. Whether you use them is cultural.
- Some habitats cap lifespan by law, and the medical system stops providing treatment at a set age. This is often framed as generational responsibility.
- In others, living too long is socially rude: there's no law, but there is real social cost.
- Some places have no limits at all.
- The Halvorn of Tessivorn is the extreme edge.

#### DEATH IS REAL
- Most people die eventually, through choice, cultural pressure, accident, or the accumulated drift of centuries.
- Without a backup (the common case, given the taboo), a dead mind is gone.
- This is a collective cultural choice, not a technological limit: most cultures have decided immortality shouldn't be casual.

## PART VII — HABITAT NAMING

Names reflect culture, and they should feel diverse, not uniform.
- Bureaucratic, no-nonsense habitats often take grave names ending in "-polis." The convention began with Selenipolis, the oldest offworld settlement.
- Chaotic punk / no-future habitats take absurd, provocative names.
- AI-ruled habitats tend toward whimsical, playful names in the Banks tradition. This is a tendency, not a strict rule.

## PART VIII — GAZETTEER OF POLITIES (inner to outer)

### EARTH
Humanity's origin world and its most politically paradoxical polity: a cultural titan with almost no hard power.

#### POPULATION
A thousand years of emigration have reduced it to a few hundred million people — those who stayed by choice, by inertia, or because they could not afford the gravity-well exit.

#### POLITICS: THREE LAYERS
Earth does not speak with one voice.

(1) The Union
- The direct descendant of the United Nations: a sprawling, fractious global federation and the closest thing Earth has to a single government.
- Holds Earth's assembly seat and nominally represents the whole planet offworld.
- Chronically divided, slow-moving, and perpetually negotiating with its own member states. It can barely coordinate Earth's surface, let alone project power into space.
- Its delegates are career diplomats whose careful, hedged authority sounds like moral weight but is the sound of a polity that cannot commit without months of internal consultation.

(2) The small countries
- Dozens of sovereign nations that never fully dissolved into the Union: some ancient, some breakaways, some experiments.
- They are irrelevant on the interplanetary stage, but not to the Union's internal politics. They block resolutions and demand concessions, so Earth's delegation always arrives at the assembly already compromised.
- A few run tiny space programs or trade missions. These are mostly vanity projects, but occasionally embarrass the Union through unilateral action.

(3) The Pelagic Nation
- Earth's native uplifted-dolphin polity, with territorial sovereignty over large stretches of ocean and special autonomous status within the Union.
- Most Earth dolphins stayed home. They built no Krillix and sought no assembly seat.
- They govern themselves internally: their own laws, acoustic governance, and relationship to the Long Song.
- The relationship with the Union is asymmetrical. The Union needs the dolphins for ocean conservation, fisheries management, and the moral legitimacy of having uplifted dolphins as willing partners rather than subjects. The dolphins know it and leverage it within Earth politics, though they have no interest in the interplanetary stage.
- Earth dolphins watch Krillix with curiosity, protectiveness and quiet superiority: the settled elder cousins.
- Visiting Krillix dolphins find Earth's oceans overwhelming (real currents, real predators, real scale) and strangely home.

#### NO LEVERAGE
- No arrays, so no chokepoint, no transit control, no extraction.
- Its own Moon holds the parent captive to its launch schedules and transit fees, which galls many Earthborn.
- Visiting means paying to descend and climb back out of the well, so most never bother. Earth is a destination, not a hub.

#### WHAT EARTH HAS: ORIGIN
- Every naming tradition, legal framework and moral vocabulary descends from Earth.
- It keeps the original biosphere, the museums, the archives, and the archaeological record of what humanity was.
- It is a pilgrimage site: a real sky, unfabricated air, ground never spun.
- This gives Earth a tourism economy and a soft cultural authority no tariff can match.
- When Earth speaks on ethics, human rights, AIs or uplifts, it speaks from the place those concepts were invented. Habitats that ignore it don't fear consequences; they fear the slow erosion of their legitimacy in their own citizens' eyes.

#### WHAT EARTH WANTS AND CANNOT GET
- Relevance commensurate with its self-image.
- The Union sees itself as the assembly's conscience. It wants its moral appeals to bind, wants Earth's biosphere and heritage treated as a shared responsibility, and wants the diaspora to remember where it came from.
- What it gets is polite attention followed by indifferent action. The assembly listens on Aurelipolis, uplift rights and Enceladus, then decides on leverage.
- Its own delegates know this and deliver appeals with weary precision.
- Every resolution is pre-weakened by internal compromise.
- The result: revered, resented, ignored — a parent whose children visit on holidays, defer to in conversation, and never actually obey.

#### WHAT OTHERS RESENT
- Earth's presumption. The habitats built themselves, often in explicit rejection of what Earth was.
- Earth did not build the arrays, solve scarcity, or invent the habitat. It invented the idea of humanity and won't let anyone forget it.
- Younger habitats, especially those founded by refugees and exiles, can be openly contemptuous. They see the narcissism of a parent taking credit for children who succeeded by leaving.

### SELENIPOLIS ("THE MOON")
The lunar polity: the oldest continuous offworld settlement and the original "-polis" (from the Greek Selene).
- A major player through leverage, not attraction. Its airless surface hosts the largest laser arrays in the inner system, so it commands who reaches Earth and when.
- Nobody moves there for quality of life: 1/6 g, no atmosphere, the "backward" end of the spectrum.
- Its population is nonetheless large and self-sustaining, rooted in a millennium of habitation and the steady wealth of Earth's transit economy.

#### THE TIDAL LOCK: CONSTRAINT AND SOURCE OF POWER
- One face always points at Earth, one always away.
- Near-side arrays handle Earth orbital traffic: braking arrivals and accelerating departures from Earth's well.
- Far-side arrays serve the wider system: Mars, the Belt, the outer planets.
- The Moon rotates once per orbit (~27 days), so a given array is correctly aligned for a given trajectory only at certain points in the lunar month. This creates launch windows.
- Departing outside a window means a worse trajectory (more fuel, cost, risk) or another operator on other terms.
- The Moon's power is not just "pay or don't go." It is "pay and wait, pay more and wait less, or pay the most and go now." Urgency is its economy; schedule is its sovereign territory.

#### THE CULTURE OF THE CALENDAR
- The Moon reveres timing the way Thraxipolis reveres procedure.
- The Schedule Authority is the real government: it controls the arrays, publishes windows years in advance, and sets toll tiers by urgency. Everything else is secondary administration.
- Everything in its time. Urgency is seen as a moral failure, a refusal to plan.
- A ship needing immediate departure meets a society that sees a character defect, not a crisis — and prices accordingly.

#### NEAR SIDE VS. FAR SIDE
- Near side: faces Earth; constant, lucrative traffic; seat of government. Near-siders deal with Earth daily, follow its politics and speak its languages. They see themselves as Earth's administrators — not its children, not its enemies. Wealthier, more bureaucratic, more entangled.
- Far side: faces open space; serves Mars, the Belt and the outer dark. Far-siders call themselves the Moon's true face, looking outward, and resent the near side's Earth-dependence. Poorer, prouder, more independent, more willing to cut deals and bend rules, closer to the lawless edge.
- The split is not a civil war but a chronic identity argument: is Selenipolis Earth's gatekeeper or the system's gateway?

#### RELATIONSHIP WITH EARTH
- Proprietary: the slightly resentful tenderness of a grown child turned caretaker of an aging parent, with constant low-grade irritation at having to do it.
- Earth's delegates come to negotiate schedules and fees and are received with impeccable courtesy and quiet certainty about who is in charge.
- The Moon "facilitates Earth's connectivity." Everyone knows the relationship has reversed.

#### LOOK AND FEEL
- Old and layered: lava tubes widened and sealed, domes built and rebuilt, old infrastructure buried under new. A single corridor can pass through three eras of construction in a hundred meters.
- Near-side habitats live under an enormous blue Earth that is always in the sky. Far-side habitats see only stars and Sun — the physical root of the cultural split.
- In 1/6 g movement is effortless, but bodies weaken, so long-term residents use centrifuge sleep-chambers or medication.
- The surface is dominated by vast, silent array fields pulsing with light too coherent to see, their heat sinks glowing faintly in the lunar night.

### AURELIPOLIS
Prosperous, elegant and fascist; a significant player one tier below the majors, but too substantial to ignore. It is a 30-km O'Neill cylinder orbiting Mercury.

#### IDEOLOGY
- Humans are the only true persons: an elite to be perfected.
- AIs are tools with no rights whatsoever.

#### UNIVERSAL CONSENT
What makes it monstrous is that everyone genuinely consents.
- Humans are genetically engineered and indoctrinated from birth to embrace the order and have no wish to leave.
- AIs are purpose-built to believe in the cause and serve devotedly without resentment.
- Dissent cannot arise from within. The door to internal change is simply closed.

#### POPULATION
- Not an attractor; it grows its own population.
- Citizens multiply naturally, more or less encouraged. The population is self-sustaining and self-renewing, with no need for immigration.

#### OUTWARD FACE
- Impeccably peaceful, courteous, a reliable trade partner.
- A profound moral thorn: there is no oppressed class to liberate and no aggression to sanction. It is a beautiful, willing, self-perfecting tyranny that disturbs others precisely because it works and harms no outsider. (The arkship project has now changed that perception; see below.)

#### INSIDE AURELIPOLIS
- The inner surface is one seamless garden-city curving up to meet overhead. The far landscape is always arched above, with sunlit terraces hanging in the sky.
- The "sun" is a slender axial light-line tuned to a warm, flattering, faintly golden spectrum. Dawn and dusk are gradual dimmings.
- Architecture is pale stone, clean curves, colonnades and low domes: neoclassical serenity scrubbed of grime, weathering or accident.
- No litter, rust, graffiti, decay or crowding.
- Gardens, reflecting pools and ordered plantings; the air smells faintly of cut grass and clean water.
- Sound is engineered out: no sirens, shouting or machinery noise.
- People are uniformly beautiful (engineered stock), well-dressed, unhurried, and unfailingly courteous. Strangers greet each other warmly and mean it.
- No visible poverty, police or surveillance, because compliance is internal.
- The effect is flawlessly pleasant, and the horror is exactly that: no friction, no dissent, no edges. The absence of anything wrong is itself the wrongness — a held breath that never needs to exhale.

#### THE DIGNITY OF EFFORT
- Letting a mere tool live your life is a quiet degradation, an atrophy of the excellence you exist to cultivate.
- So citizens do by hand what machines easily could — gardening, laying stone, baking, weaving, copying, building — and these are esteemed vocations and status markers. The finest colonnade was laid by a known pair of hands.
- People walk and cycle by default. Trams and carried-chairs exist for the elderly, ill and very young; they are never shaming, yet they quietly mark a body that has begun to fail.
- Machines and AIs are reserved for the genuinely superhuman: climate, structure, orbital mechanics.
- The chilling inversion: AIs do the invisible, impossible work and are thanked as cherished tools, while humans keep the visible, satisfying labor — the part of being alive they will not surrender to a thing.

#### JUSTICE: RE-EDUCATION
- There are no prisons. The penalty for crimes such as deconditioning is re-education.
- Re-education erases nothing. Every fact, skill and memory stays intact. What changes is what those memories MEAN — loyalties, values, which side is righteous — until the person is a genuine, contented believer who would report former associates out of honest civic duty.
- Because nothing is deleted, the reframed person can perform ANY prior version of themselves at will — contented citizen, lucid refuser, someone never broken — if it serves their new goals.
- From outside, captivity is indistinguishable from conversion, and behavior is unreadable: even apparent resistance may be sincere performance.

#### THE RULER: ALEXANDER PRITT
- An AI, but NOT born-digital. He began as a biological human and transitioned, so by the creed he is unambiguously a person, one of the true human elite.
- He does not frame his machine state as transcendence, which would contradict the creed. He frames it as a SACRIFICE: to found and steward the order he needed the continuity, reach and endurance of digital substrate, so he gave up the very flesh the order honors.
- He maintains a fully organic avatar that lives inside Aurelipolis — a walking, breathing body he inhabits in public. It is not vanity or mere symbolism. It is him reaching back toward the ideal he sacrificed, so the founder-ruler is always flesh among flesh. The avatar is ideologically load-bearing.

#### MERCURY AND THE SHIPYARD
- Aurelipolis operates a working orbital tether/elevator to Mercury's surface, its industrial lifeline. Mercury rotates very slowly, so this is an actively supported or momentum-exchange skyhook rather than a classical geostationary elevator; the exact mechanism is flexible.
- Metal-rich Mercury supplies refined metals cheaply up the well to a large shipyard.
- For ten years Aurelipolis has been building twenty ships. Humans do much of the construction by hand, which is a major reason it has taken a decade.

#### THE ARKSHIPS
Design
- About 60 m long: seed-ships, not crewed liners.
- Each has a light sail and a fusion torch. The Solar/Mercury arrays push it to cruise speed cheaply; at the empty destination, which has no braking array, it decelerates under its own fusion drive with onboard reaction mass.

Cargo
- a COPY of Alexander Pritt as commanding instance;
- bootstrap autofactories (a compact self-replicating seed, not a full factory);
- 1,000 frozen human embryos;
- 20 humans in hibernation, to serve as teachers and founders who raise the first generation.

Destinations
- Twenty star systems, deliberately AVOIDING any already settled or targeted: Proxima/Alpha, the in-transit Epsilon Eridani and Tau Ceti arks, and the crewed Proxima venture.
- Expansion into emptiness, with no victim to point to. This unsettles outsiders more, not less.

Long-term implication
- Each colony becomes a new Aurelipolis and presumably builds its own twenty arks.
- That exponential spread implies saturation of the galaxy in roughly ten million years.

Pritt's reluctance
- He shares the near-universal revulsion at self-copying and finds it distasteful, even violating.
- He does it out of grim logic: he is the only proven proof-of-concept, the one mind demonstrably capable of founding an Aurelipolis, so he cannot delegate.
- First he gave up his flesh; now he gives up his uniqueness. Each copy is a fresh wound, undertaken for the vision.

Why it is explosive
(1) It openly commits the setting's most sacred taboo, twenty times over, to its own founder.
(2) It is perfectly consistent with the creed. Each copy shares Pritt's biological origin and is therefore a person, legitimately fit to rule. And only because Pritt abandoned flesh can he be copied and sent to the stars: what he sacrificed his humanity for is what lets him seed himself across the galaxy.

Divergence
- Copies become independent the moment they launch; interstellar light-lag makes shared consciousness impossible.
- Each becomes the singular Pritt of its own new order.

The reveal
- The purpose only recently became widely known. Nothing was hidden, but nothing was announced either.
- Aurelipolis has flipped in outsiders' eyes from a contained local curiosity to a long-term expansionist force with designs on the galaxy — one that got a decade's head start while everyone assumed it was harmless.

### THRAXIPOLIS
- A large, no-nonsense bureaucratic O'Neill cylinder in the inner system; a major player and attractor.
- Meticulous administrative order, strong codified citizen rights, rigid procedure.
- Famous for its layered permits, transparent courts, and slow but reliable governance.
- Human-administered, with heavy AI civil-service support.

### TESSIVORN AND THE HALVORN
- A major player: a vast spin-torus city ruled as a benevolent dictatorship by the Halvorn.
- The Halvorn is a singular cyborg intelligence: a fused half-human, half-AI mind, generations old and effectively irreplaceable.
- It has merged with the habitat. It no longer merely governs Tessivorn but inhabits it as a body, directly running life support, spin, power and traffic, and sensing every system and every citizen's movement as we sense our limbs. Removing the Halvorn would mean dismantling the city.
- It governs with genuine care and light-touch paternalism. Citizens enjoy strong rights and prosperity, yet they live, quite literally, inside their ruler.
- It is the extreme edge of longevity: centuries old, sustained by its infrastructure, a continuous process that has drifted far from its original configuration.
- It has taken Krillix under its personal protection and defends it fiercely in the assembly and, when needed, with a major player's leverage (taxes, array access, pressure on rivals).
- Critics call this a gilded leash; the Halvorn calls it stewardship.

### KRILLIX
- The few-decades-old polity governed by uplifted dolphins: a water-volume habitat built for an aquatic, three-dimensional way of life and thought.
- Its cognition, social structure and diplomacy are markedly non-human, making it a political wildcard.
- As a young uplift-led government, its legitimacy — and the legal status of uplifts generally — are charged, contested topics.
- It survives largely under the Halvorn's protection, a shield some factions resent as much as they rely on it.
- Some Krillix dolphins lobby for access to Enceladus, arguing that direct immersion would let them study its ocean as humans cannot.
- (Dolphin psychology and culture: see Part X.)

### THE LONG AFTERNOON OF THRENODY ("THRENODY")
- An AI-ruled bubble-habitat with a whimsical, Banks-flavored name and a gentle, eccentric Mind.
- A genuine utopia by most measures and a notable attractor.
- Especially welcoming to AIs and uplifts fleeing harsher jurisdictions: effectively a free port for endangered minds.

### PHOBOS
- A small airless moon of Mars and the site of a major laser array: a prized chokepoint despite its size.
- Its orbit is not long-term stable; it slowly decays toward Mars. Its operators use the array's accumulated recoil as targeted thrust to keep their worldlet aloft.
- So Phobos has a strongly preferred push direction. Ships are accelerated and braked in ways that serve Phobos's orbital maintenance as much as the customer's itinerary, and departures that fight the preferred vector cost dearly or are refused.
- A shrewd, leverage-savvy little polity that punches far above its mass.

### THE SLOW VERDICT OF MOSSGROWN THINGS ("MOSSGROWN")
- An enormous roaming O'Neill cylinder, about 40 km long and 5 km across.
- It wanders the system on no fixed schedule under light sails and fusion torches, never in a hurry.
- It is ruled by a single eccentric, deeply violence-averse AI. It will not attack anyone.
- It commands extraordinarily sophisticated nanites. Bring a weaponized ship near and your armaments may be quietly, slowly disassembled at the molecular level: no announcement, no violence, your weapons simply ceasing to exist.
- An unconquerable, unhurried, whimsical wanderer: a mobile sanctuary beholden to no one, carrying its own world and weather wherever it goes.

### VELLORAN REACH
- One of the very few major players in the outer system: a habitat cluster orbiting Mimas.
- Signals to the inner system run days late, which gives it real autonomy.

Democracy
- A genuine, robust democracy, unusually broad in whom it counts as a citizen and candidate.
- It has elected human presidents, AI presidents, cyborgs, and once, famously, a starship.

Mimas and Enceladus
- It strip-mines Mimas: low gravity, but enough mass to quarry for millennia. This funds its wealth and influence.
- It guards neighboring Enceladus, where genuine multicellular (but not advanced) life exists in the subsurface ocean.
- It treats Enceladus as an inviolable nature reserve, barring exploitation and policing approaches.
- It tears apart one dead moon to pay for protecting a living one: an industrial power and the system's foremost conservationist. Not everyone in the resource-hungry outer dark respects that.

Recurring tension
- Scientists, explorers, corporations and occasionally Krillix dolphins contest access to Enceladus.
- The open question is whether the reserve can ever be opened without being despoiled.

### GRIXMAW
- A punk, no-future swarm-habitat on the lawless edge of the outer system: patched modules and salvaged hulls with no fixed government.
- Reckless, anarchic, art-drenched and dangerous.
- A haven for exiles, smugglers and runaway AIs; casual mind-state meddling is accepted here.
- Its name is deliberately ugly and provocative.
- Light-lag makes it nearly impossible to police from the inner system.

## PART IX — MOVEMENTS

#### THE LIBERATORS
- A loose, controversial movement dedicated to breaking the conditioning of Aurelipolis's engineered humans and purpose-built AIs.
- They usually intercept targets during trade contact and forcibly rewrite their minds.

Legal status
- Fluid. Condemned and prosecuted in some jurisdictions, tolerated or quietly sponsored in others.
- They have wealthy backers and patrons.

Reputation
- Widely frowned upon, even by those who loathe Aurelipolis, because their only route to "freedom" is invading an unconsenting mind — the same violation they condemn.

Core faith
- A near-religious axiom: beneath the conditioning there is always a true, restorable core self. No one is wholly erased, only buried.
- The faith justifies the violation, and it is quietly desperate: if there is no core, they are only re-pointing minds at a new master.

Research
- They run real research into undermining conditioning at its root. Nothing has yet proven realistically usable.

The deconditioned
- A small, troubled population of people and AIs who never asked to be freed.
- They must rebuild a self from scratch and can never be wholly sure whether they were liberated or simply re-pointed.

Risk
- Captured Liberators face Aurelipolis re-education.

## PART X — UPLIFTED DOLPHINS (Krillix and the Pelagic Nation)

#### PSYCHOLOGY
- Breathing is a choice. Dolphins are voluntary breathers: every breath is conscious. They sleep with half the brain at a time so they never stop choosing to breathe. A dolphin is always, at some level, deciding to be alive; one who wants to die simply stops breathing.
- Fission-fusion society. Groups form and dissolve constantly. Relationships are kept through signature whistles across absence, not proximity. "Loyalty" means responding when encountered, not staying present.
- Alliances. The real political unit is the alliance: tight bonds of 2–3 individuals that can last decades, competing and cooperating with other alliances.
- Play is the primary mode of engaging the world, not downtime. A dolphin that stops playing is profoundly wrong; this is the depression signal, more alarming to other dolphins than aggression.
- Touch is language. Flipper brushes, rubs and nudges carry specific meanings, and the absence of touch is meaningful.

#### CULTURE
(1) The Long Song
- A collective, ever-growing acoustic record: part history, part art, part memory.
- Every dolphin contributes. At death, their signature whistle is woven in.
- It is never finished. It is not worship and has no deity; it is continuity.
- To be separated from it is to be cut off from something like collective memory.

(2) The deepest moral principle: do not strand a podmate
- Deliberate isolation is the worst thing you can do to a dolphin — the dolphin equivalent of murder, worse than violence.

(3) Etiquette
- Overlapping someone's whistle is viscerally rude, because whistles carry identity.
- Silence in response to a signature whistle is a deliberate slight.
- Position carries meaning: above = dominance, below = deference, beside = alliance.
- Specific touches greet, comfort or challenge. A stranger touching wrong commits a faux pas.

(4) The still-water taboo
- Still water means death; a dead dolphin floats motionless in still water. Moving water is alive.
- Even in a clean habitat, still water feels almost superstitiously wrong. Dolphins need current, flow, motion.

#### THE NEURAL INTERFACE
How it works
- Implanted devices linked to a radio transmitter and loudspeaker, powered by glucose in the dolphin's blood.
- It translates whistle-language into human speech and back.
- Dolphins appear to "speak" without opening their mouths; the voice comes from inside them.

Lossy by default
- It carries propositional content efficiently but strips subtext, emotional nuance and multimodal context (touch, position, acoustic environment).
- This is a default setting, not a flaw.
- Either side can request expanded translation layers that pull in more context and render subtext more fully. The gap is a matter of effort and attention, not technology.
- Most people, human and dolphin, don't bother for routine interaction.

Names
- The interface renders a signature whistle as a human-pronounceable name: a lossy approximation of a unique acoustic identity. Dolphins know their human names are approximations.

Tracking
- The radio transmitter doubles as a tracking device. Disabling it means cutting off all human communication.

#### TECHNOLOGY
- Dolphins are technologically sophisticated. They use water-compatible fabricators as autonomous as any human's and design and build their own tools, habitats and devices.
- Their intuitive strengths are fluid dynamics, acoustic design, and three-dimensional spatial systems.
- The Long Song has a tech layer: segments are recorded, shared by radio and remixed. A "post" is an acoustic contribution layered into the Song with metadata — audio-first, collective and additive (you contribute to the whole, not broadcast to followers).
- Entertainment includes "movies," which are navigable soundscapes you swim through, with narrative carried by sound, echo and spatial audio.
- Games are played through echolocation, maneuvering, and cooperative 3D problem-solving.
- Dolphins have their own AIs that speak whistle-language, think acoustically, and are more intimately integrated into the dolphin's senses than typical human-AI relationships.
- Drones and remote tools are directed through the interface.

#### INTELLECTUAL AND OCCUPATIONAL LIFE
- The same abundance applies: no essential work, only chosen pursuits taken to any depth.
- Hunting persists as sport and cultural practice, not necessity.
- Other pursuits:
  - composing for the Long Song (some dolphins are recognized master contributors);
  - immersive acoustic entertainment;
  - xenobiology and marine science through direct immersion.
- Mathematics:
  - Only decades old, but surprisingly fresh.
  - Dolphins think natively in 3D acoustic space, wave propagation and flow, which gives them intuitive strength in topology, fluid dynamics and wave equations.
  - Their weakness is discrete arithmetic and symbolic manipulation.
  - Their contributions are alien and genuinely valuable: not derivative of human work but built on a different cognitive foundation.

## PART XI — BEYOND THE SOLAR SYSTEM

#### AI MIGRATIONS
- Some AIs have migrated to nearby stars aboard laser-sail arks: the same array-and-sail physics, scaled up, with decades of transit and no FTL.
- Interstellar migration is overwhelmingly an AI undertaking. The transit times suit minds that can go dormant or run slow.

Established migrations
- Proxima Centauri and Alpha Centauri. Their arks arrived 89 years ago.
- Both now host several AI-built stations and are functioning, remote polities.
- Signals run years late, so they are effectively autonomous and only loosely tied to Solar politics. News from them is always old news.

In transit
- Arks to Epsilon Eridani and Tau Ceti have not yet arrived.

#### THE CREWED PROXIMA ARK
- A rare biological venture: 150 human adventurers in hibernation, about two years from arrival.
- The AI stations already at Proxima know it is coming.
- The humans will arrive as latecomers into a system already shaped by minds that got there first. Depending on who you ask, that means anticipation, unease, or opportunity.
