# Solar-system background

The Outside view now shares a natural solar-system model with the existing habitat frame tree. The reference clock remains 3026. The Solar system catalogue opens an observer view of each world; Return to habitat restores the cylinder view. Distances and body sizes are never enlarged to make a miniature system. The observer can accelerate the clock, up to the offline ephemeris boundary, where it pauses.

## Contents and sources

- Sun, eight planets, the five recognised dwarf planets, Vesta, Pallas and Hygiea.
- All 461 planetary satellites in the [JPL discovery catalogue](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/discovery.html) snapshot used here, plus Dysnomia, Hiʻiaka, Namaka and MK 2. Mercury, Venus and Ceres have no invented moons. The [JPL mean-elements tables](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/sep.html) contain Puck twice: one body uses its later fit. S/2009 S 1 is absent from those fitted tables; its circular ring-plane approximation uses the distance and inferred size reported in [IAUC 9091](https://tamkin3.eps.harvard.edu/iauc/09000/09091.html).
- Major-body radii and flattening follow [JPL physical parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html). Moon radii use [JPL's satellite parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/) and Horizons object records where available. Unknown small-moon radii use an explicitly labelled 1 km placeholder, not a claimed measurement. Dwarf-moon sizes and orbits are approximate; MK 2's geometry and phase are particularly uncertain.
- [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html) / DE441 geometric vectors supply the Sun-relative planetary-system barycentres and Earth's geocentric Moon for 3020–3080. The Earth–Moon mass ratio separates Earth from its barycentre. Other planetary centres approximate their system barycentres; large-moon reflex displacements are not yet included. Moon positions use the parent centre, never the heliocentric origin.
- [JPL SBDB](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/sbdb.html) elements supply Ceres, Haumea, Makemake, Eris and the three named asteroids. Horizons does not provide their integrations after 2500, so these use two-body propagation with documented uncertainty, rather than claiming precise future ephemerides.

`tools/space/import.py` refreshes the checked-in data. It requires Python, numpy and BeautifulSoup, makes sequential public-service requests, and caches responses under `.render/astronomy`. The browser loads only `public/space/*.json`, about 4.5 MB uncompressed, when Outside starts. It never calls JPL. The import stores independent validation vectors in `test/fixtures/solar-vectors.json`. Additional small-moon sizes derived from published absolute magnitude assume geometric albedo 0.04 and remain labelled estimates. S/2025 U 1 uses the approximately 10 km diameter in the [NASA/Webb discovery announcement](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/webb/2025/08/19/new-moon-discovered-orbiting-uranus-using-nasas-webb-telescope/).

## Accuracy and model limits

Coordinates are float64 heliocentric J2000 ecliptic metres. The injected uniform clock is treated as TDB for these vectors; its millisecond-scale difference from TT is immaterial here. Light-time, aberration, relativistic travel and spacecraft navigation are not simulated. The planetary pack fits degree-12 Chebyshev segments; every other input sample is withheld for validation. Maximum measured interpolation errors are below 0.8 km for the planets and below 3 m for the geocentric Moon. These are errors against the downloaded integration, not claims about the certainty of actual positions a millennium from now.

JPL explicitly warns that satellite mean elements describe orbit shapes, not high-fidelity ephemerides. Their reference planes (ecliptic, equatorial or local Laplace), epochs, eccentricities, inclinations, periods and precession periods are preserved. Extrapolated phases and resonance details are approximate in 3026. The catalogue says so. Dwarf moons with poorly constrained orientations use illustrative equatorial orbits. This is a travel background, not a mission-planning package.

Planet mean poles, axial flattening and rotations orient the bodies and rings. Surface appearances are inexpensive procedural colour fields: recognisable clouds, belts, ice and rock, not surveyed terrain. Earth and the Moon retain their existing maps. Surface landing and high-resolution terrain are outside this feature. The existing reference Necklace remains; no new habitats or planetary settlements have been added. World-bible polities and industrial changes to moons can be layered onto this natural baseline later.

## Asteroid populations

`space/asteroids.ts` deterministically addresses individual objects by seed, population and index. Model populations are 1.2 million main-belt objects and 0.5 million Jupiter Trojans above 1 km diameter, plus 70,000 classical Kuiper objects above 100 km. Counts and size-law exponents are statistical choices, not a catalogue of measured objects.

The main belt spans 2.1–3.3 AU, with Rayleigh eccentricity and inclination distributions and strong depletion near Jupiter's 3:1, 5:2, 7:3 and 2:1 resonances. Gaps are in semi-major axis: eccentric orbits cross their instantaneous radii, as explained by the [Minor Planet Center](https://docs.minorplanetcenter.net/tutorials/notebooks/mpc_tutorial_orbit_kirkwood/). Composition favours darker carbonaceous material farther out. The [NASA space-flight overview](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter1-3/) describes the belt's structure and broad composition classes.

Trojan mean longitudes librate around Jupiter's leading and trailing 60° regions, with inclined and eccentric orbits. This is a statistical libration approximation, not a restricted-three-body integration. Kuiper objects occupy 42–48 AU with cold and hot inclination components. The renderer samples the largest 1,024 procedural objects; the rest are addressable by the model for future encounter streaming. It does not fill the player's vicinity with invented nearby rocks. Scattered-disc populations, comets and small-body satellite systems beyond the named dwarf moons are future extensions.

## Rendering and ownership

`SolarView` uses a fixed pool of twenty shared sphere/ring pairs and one point buffer. Subpixel bodies retain reflected-light flux and phase; larger bodies retain their true angular size. Positions are subtracted in float64 before their finite render-depth projection. Parent eclipses use the finite solar disc; rings shade their planets and receive their planet's shadow. Ring bands distinguish broad Saturnian rings from the narrow Uranian/Neptunian systems. The Sun's radiance and surface illumination obey the inverse-square relationship.

The pool is prepared through Stage, shares two registry materials, sheds the procedural population under memory pressure, reports its data/buffer ownership, and releases uploaded static arrays. Body geometry disposal uses `freeMesh`. The observer has a 200 AU free-camera range; it does not move any habitat. The frame tree remains the seam for future travel, with `SolarSystem.position` and `velocity` providing the natural-body states.
